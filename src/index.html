The idea

Brace with one hand.
Work with the other.

Interaction forces can disturb a humanoid’s balance. SHS uses a supporting-hand contact to help resist those forces, while a separate working-arm policy follows the position–force task.

Video description

The presentation introduces the balance challenge in forceful manipulation, then explains how an offline optimiser selects one support configuration for an entire task region. A whole-body keypoint reference guides synchronous body and working-arm policies. Hardware demonstrations show support establishment, fixed-position force tests, and figure-eight tracking at 25, 40, and 55 N. Results compare SHS with a task-independent Generic reference and the Modular hardware baseline. Force arrows and overlays are visual annotations; the paper defines the evaluation metrics.

The method

One support configuration.
A whole region of work.

Task-conditioned support is optimised offline. During execution, two learned policies coordinate the working arm and the rest of the body synchronously.

01 / OPTIMISE

Find the brace

The Task-Region Support Optimiser (TRSO) finds one shared body and supporting-arm configuration across working-hand position, normal, and force samples. SLSQP returns a local solution.

02 / REPRESENT

Encode the geometry

The Whole-Body Keypoint Representation (WBKR) encodes an 18D support reference in a heading frame fixed at task activation. The working hand follows its own command.

03 / EXECUTE

Coordinate two policies

A 7-joint working-arm policy and a 22-joint body policy run synchronously. The left hand maintains support; the right hand tracks the position–force target.

From reference to contact

Learn to establish
and maintain support.

Both policies first learn on static targets. The body policy is then frozen while the working-arm policy is trained on continuous position–force trajectories.

The learned policies receive target force. Measured wrist force is used for hardware evaluation.

What makes bracing work?

The experiments separate environmental contact, reference guidance, and task conditioning across 30 independently trained runs per method, paired by seed.

Bracing improves success.
The reference matters, too.

SHS raises mean stable-contact hold ratio from 0.069 to 0.950 relative to No-bracing. Against Generic, force MAE falls by 3.02 N (26.9%), with similar position MAE.

How is simulation success defined?

An episode must establish a stable-contact window, achieve mean position error ≤ 5 cm and force MAE ≤ 10 N within that window, and avoid early termination. Success is averaged over all held-out episodes. Hold ratio is averaged over episodes that establish a stable-contact window.

Abstract

Forceful manipulation is challenging for humanoid robots because interaction forces can disturb whole-body balance. We introduce the Supporting Hand Strategy (SHS), which enables a humanoid to brace against the environment with one hand while performing forceful manipulation with the other. SHS optimises a task-conditioned support configuration that guides two synchronous reinforcement-learning policies, without human motion data or online whole-body trajectory planning. On a Unitree G1, SHS achieved usable contact forces up to 60 N, compared with a maximum sustained force of 13.5 N without environmental bracing, while substantially improving force tracking over a task-independent support configuration. The same policies generalised to different task regions without retraining. SHS therefore provides a simple mechanism for substantially extending humanoid forceful-manipulation capability.

Abstract reproduced from the manuscript. Transfer uses newly optimised support references; the 13.5 N comparison is a one-second sustained-force limit.
