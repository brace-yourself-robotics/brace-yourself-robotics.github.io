The idea

Brace with one hand.
Work with the other.

Interaction forces can disturb a humanoid’s balance. The Supporting Hand Strategy (SHS) braces one hand against the environment, so the other can track a target position and force.

No human motion data

No online whole-body trajectory planning

No measured force input to the RL policies

The method

One support configuration.
A whole region of work.

Task-conditioned support is optimised offline. During execution, two learned policies coordinate the working arm and the rest of the body synchronously.

01 / OPTIMISE

Find the brace

The Task-Region Support Optimiser (TRSO) evaluates every working-hand position and force target in the task region together and returns one shared support configuration: foot placement, forward lean, waist and supporting-arm posture. Balance, friction and ZMP are checked offline, so nothing is replanned during the task.

02 / REPRESENT

Encode the geometry

The Whole-Body Keypoint Representation (WBKR) turns this configuration into an 18D reference: a pelvis support vector from the feet, plus torso, shoulder, supporting elbow and hand positions relative to the pelvis. The working arm is left out so it stays free for the task.

03 / EXECUTE

Coordinate two policies

The body policy (legs, waist, supporting arm) tracks the WBKR reference and holds the brace. The working-arm policy tracks the position and force command. Both read the same observation and act at the same time, with the target force as input but no measured force.

On hardware

Establish support.
Then execute the task.

Starting from standing, the body policy leans the robot forward and places the supporting hand on the wall, following the WBKR reference. With the brace in place, the working-arm policy brings the other hand to the task surface and tracks the position and force command.

Simulation ablations

What makes bracing work?

Simulation removes environmental bracing, reference guidance or task conditioning one at a time. Each method is trained 30 times with independent seeds, paired across methods.

Bracing improves success.
The reference matters, too.

SHS raises mean stable-contact hold ratio from 0.069 to 0.950 relative to No-bracing. Against Generic, force MAE falls by 3.02 N (26.9%), with similar position MAE.

How is simulation success defined?

An episode must establish a stable-contact window, achieve mean position error ≤ 5 cm and force MAE ≤ 10 N within that window, and avoid early termination. Success is averaged over all held-out episodes. Hold ratio is averaged over episodes that establish a stable-contact window.

The paper

Brace Yourself

Task-Conditioned Environmental Bracing for Forceful Humanoid Manipulation

Read the full paper ↗

Submitted to IEEE Robotics and Automation Letters (RA-L) · 8 pages · PDF

Abstract

Forceful manipulation is challenging for humanoid robots because interaction forces can disturb whole-body balance. We introduce the Supporting Hand Strategy (SHS), which enables a humanoid to brace against the environment with one hand while performing forceful manipulation with the other. SHS optimises a task-conditioned support configuration that guides two synchronous reinforcement-learning policies, without human motion data or online whole-body trajectory planning. On a Unitree G1, SHS achieved usable contact forces up to 60 N, compared with a maximum sustained force of 13.5 N without environmental bracing, while substantially improving force tracking over a task-independent support configuration. The same policies generalised to different task regions without retraining. SHS therefore provides a simple mechanism for substantially extending humanoid forceful-manipulation capability.
